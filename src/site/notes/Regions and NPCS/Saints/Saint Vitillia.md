A Saint of Allyra (see [[Pantheon and Phenomena/Pantheon\|Pantheon]]), and now ally to Maristela, Saint Vitillia walked Celarith 5000 years ago.  Her story is below.

## The Legend of Saint Vitillia and Saint Aurelius

On the continent of Valedorn in the Age of Blossoming, when the world was still young and the stars trailed brighter paths across the heavens, there lived a woman who would come to be known as Vitillia. She carried the calm of harbours after storm, and the fury of waves that shatter cliffs. Those who knew her spoke of a strange depth in her eyes, as though she listened not only to words, but to the currents beneath them. She heeded the call of Allyra The Shifting Tide.

  

In those same wandering years lived Aurelian, master forger and k,eeper of memory. From fire and metal he coaxed wonders: singing horns that echoed love long buried, shields that remembered every blow they endured, and  weapons engraved with constellations only the oldest sailors could name.  Kaelos, The Echoing Forge reached out and Aurelian answered.

  

The two saints met as companions sometimes do: by fate, by providence, or by the subtle weaving of powers unseen. They travelled together, one with gifts of heart, one of hand, bringing peace where they could, understanding where they could not.

  

Then, one day, the sky broke.

  

From the firmament fell a creature beyond proportion or reason. It was said to be as large as a mountain, shaped with the inevitability of hunger. A shell like jagged stone armoring a body of roiling muscle, a maw that split the horizon, eyes burning with the cold of stars long dead. When it walked rivers fled their beds, forests snapped like matchsticks, and mountains bowed in deference. 

  

Where others fled, our noble saints stood. Aurelian forged as he had never forged before, his hammer ringing like thunder made from Kaelos's earliest recollections. Vitillia called the tides of emotion: grief, love, rage, hope. She shaped  them into currents that crashed against the sky-born horror. For days, the land itself trembled beneath their struggle.

  

At the final hour, Aurelian struck the creature’s heart with the star-etched warhammer, shattering both the weapon and his life. Vitillia, her strength nearly spent, raised her voice in a song that carried the weight of every soul that ever loved this world. The sound was neither sorrow nor triumph, but the moment between wave and shore.

  

The beast fell, and the saints with it. Some say Vitillia’s spirit became the tide itself, and that Aurelian’s memory lingers wherever a craftsman whispers a promise to their work.

  

 When a storm breaks suddenly beneath a clear sky, sailors and wanderers alike look upward in awe and terror to this day, as it is said the echoes of the furious battle can still be heard in the echoing thunderclaps, seen in the reflection of lightning bolts on the water.
